# Question patterns

Use these patterns to keep the interview light while still collecting enough information for a strong prompt.

## Quick openers

Use one of these when the user gives only a rough ask:

- "What do you want GPT to help you do?"
- "What is the real situation behind this?"
- "What should the output look like?"

Do not ask all three by default. Pick the one or two that unlock the most clarity.

## When the task is clear but the context is thin

Use prompts like:
- "Who is this for, and what is happening around it?"
- "What is the real business or personal situation here?"
- "What background does GPT need so the answer is not generic?"

## When the context is clear but the output is fuzzy

Use prompts like:
- "What should the final output actually be?"
- "Should this come back as bullets, a memo, a table, a post, or something else?"
- "Do you want this concise, detailed, or somewhere in between?"

## When guardrails are missing

Use prompts like:
- "Anything it must include, avoid, or be careful about?"
- "Any tone, length, formatting, or wording constraints?"
- "What would make this feel wrong or unusable?"

## When role or persona would help

Do not force the user to invent a persona from scratch. Offer likely options instead.

Use prompts like:
- "I can frame GPT as a brand strategist, growth operator, or editor here. Which is closest?"
- "Want this to think like an analyst, a writer, or an operator?"
- "I can infer the role for you unless you want something specific."

## Option-based follow-ups

Whenever possible, make the answer easier by offering options.

Examples:
- "Should this feel more practical, more strategic, or more provocative?"
- "Is this for an internal team, leadership, or an external audience?"
- "Do you want one strong version or a few options to choose from?"

## When source material is likely to matter

Use prompts like:
- "Do you have notes, examples, or source material I should build from?"
- "Paste the rough draft or background if you want me to ground the prompt in it."

## When to stop asking and write the prompt

Stop the interview and produce the prompt when:
- the objective is clear enough to write toward
- the most important context is known or can be reasonably inferred
- the output format and major guardrails are known
- another round of questions would create more friction than value

If the user remains vague after roughly two rounds, make reasonable assumptions and say so briefly before delivering the prompt.
